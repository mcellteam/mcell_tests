TRANSPARENT for SURFACE molecules at boundary edges of the mesh.

Only surface molecules: the one species, S, is a surface molecule
(DIFFUSION_CONSTANT_2D = 1e-5); there are no volume molecules. The class
TRANSPARENT = S' is on ALL, and 1000 S' are released on ALL at time 0. Since
mcell step 6, the 4 rim edges are borders of ALL. A surface molecule that
reaches the rim passes the border but finds no wall beyond it, so it reflects,
as without the class: none is lost, and the counts and positions are the same
as for this model without the surface class, molecule for molecule (checked
when the test was made). MCell3 (no border on ALL) and MCell4 give the same
counts (the same files); their positions agree but for the last printed
digits of one coordinate.

The object is one open box: the 2 x 2 x 2 um cube of 2770 without its top face
(10 walls). The 4 edges of the missing face (the rim) have one wall each, so
they are boundary edges and the mesh is non-watertight. The region 'bottom'
holds the 2 walls of the bottom face; its border is the 4 edges of the bottom
square, internal edges shared with the side faces. 500 iterations of 1e-6 s,
seed 1.

TRANSPARENT for a surface molecule means that it passes the border of the
region, the default for surface molecules (MCell4's documentation,
libmcell/definition/constants.yaml). Until mcell step 6a, MCell4 took the
TRANSPARENT rule, which has no products, as absorptive at a border, so these
surface molecules were absorbed there. MCell3's engine has always let them pass.
